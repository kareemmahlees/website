I was the lead software engineer at Thyab, a Saudi-Based startup providing a fully-fledged ERP system specifically aimed towards digitalizing the tailoring industry, it provides a wide-variety of solutions that simplifies and modernizes the tailoring process which was previously time consuming and no way near convenient.

Being a startup we were constrained by budget and how much we can spend, that's were I came into play by optimizations, in-house implementations and self-hosting:

- WhatsApp: I migrated our WhatsApp integration from a 3rd party service that was limiting, expensive and slow to our own in-house implementation written in Golang which was faster, far more customizable and saving the company around 14k SAR per year.
- InfraStructure: I self-hosted our entire infrastructure on a dedicated VPS instead of hosting on Vercel, which unlocked more CI/CD capabilities such as preview-deployments, multi-environment workflows and saving the company 40 USD a month.

I was responsible for securing our entire infrastructure by utilizing modern practices such as firewalls, proxies and cloudflare tunnels which proves resilience when tested and attacked by a cybersecurity engineer.

Thyab being a software used by different types of employees for 8+ hours on daily bases and hundreds of orders made in peak times, I played a crucial role in ensuring that the system could handle the load and still be responsive, fast and also not burn our costs.

I also worked closely with sales representatives and went into meetings with customers to understand their complaints and collect feedback on how to improve our product.

Being a startup also meant that we made mistakes and we learn from it, not only business side but also technical side, to name a few of what I was responsible for (most where done pre-AI):

- I migrated our infrastructure from a Hostinger VPS to a Hetzner VPS.
- I migrated our codebase from Nextjs to Tanstack-Start
- I migrated our authentication system from authjs to bette-auth.
- I introduced radical changes to our database architecture which required incremental data-migrations
